How it works

How the arcade is built

  • One interface, eight gamesEvery cabinet talks to its game through one small CabinetGame interface: give me a texture, take these keys, tell me when to flash. The cabinet does the rest.
  • Cartridges loaded on demandSeven games are the cartridges from my website, each a plain script with the same engine API. A cartridge is only fetched when you walk up to its cabinet, and only the selected cabinet runs its game. The rest show cover art.
  • A Python game, portedMini Knight started as a Pygame Zero game. It's ported line by line to TypeScript and drawn as its own Three.js scene with an orthographic camera into a WebGLRenderTarget.
  • A 3D game inside a 3D gameNeon Run renders its own Three.js scene off-screen every frame, and that picture becomes the texture on a tube inside this scene. It was written for three.js r128, so it gets a small shim that converts its old light units.
  • CRT shaderA GLSL ShaderMaterial samples that texture with barrel curvature, a slight RGB convergence error, phosphor glow, one scanline per game row, an aperture-grille mask and a vignette. Toggle CRT to compare.
  • HDR bloomThe screen, the marquee and the neon trim render brighter than white, so UnrealBloomPass picks up only those before tone mapping. Toggle Bloom to compare.
  • Raycast controlsClicks are raycast against the curved glass. The hit's UV becomes a game coordinate, so you can click the game's own START, SOUND and EXIT buttons on the screen.
  • Blender to glTFThe cabinet loads models/cabinet.glb when it's there. A mesh named Screen gets the CRT material; Joystick and the buttons move with the game.
